## The meeting cycle

Meeting features in OpenProject support the entire meeting cycle, from initial preparation to documenting decisions and following up on action items. Whether you are organizing a one-time meeting or a recurring meeting series, the following steps will help you prepare, run and follow up on your meetings.

### 1. Prepare your meeting

Before creating a meeting, consider its purpose, participants and agenda structure.

If you frequently organize meetings with a similar structure, consider using a [meeting template](meeting-templates/) to reuse agenda sections, items and attachments. Recurring meeting series have their own templates, which provide a consistent starting point for future meetings.

### 2. Create your meeting

[Create a meeting](meeting-creation/) from within a project, from the global Meetings module or using the **+** button in the header navigation. Choose between a one-time meeting and a recurring meeting series.

New meetings initially open in [draft mode](meeting-modes/#meeting-draft-mode). This allows you to prepare the meeting before making it available to participants.

### 3. Prepare the agenda

Build your [meeting agenda](meeting-agenda/) by adding sections, agenda items, work packages and relevant information. Invite [participants](meeting-participants/) and collaborate on the agenda before the meeting starts.

You can organize agenda items manually or use voting to prioritize topics for discussion.

### 4. Run your meeting

When you are ready to begin, update the [meeting status](meeting-modes/#change-meeting-status) and use [presentation mode](meeting-modes/#present-a-meeting) to guide participants through the agenda.

During the meeting, take notes, update agenda items and document [meeting outcomes](meeting-outcomes/), such as decisions and follow-up tasks.

### 5. Follow up after the meeting

After the meeting, review the documented outcomes and make sure follow-up tasks are captured.

You can [export the meeting as a PDF](meeting-follow-up/#export-a-meeting) to share the meeting record or use the meeting history to review changes.

For recurring meetings, you can move or duplicate agenda items that need further discussion into the next meeting occurrence, connecting one meeting to the next.
